About TFBSpedia

What TFBSpedia is

TFBSpedia is a database of transcription factor binding sites (TFBS) in the human and mouse genomes. Transcription factors control when and where genes are switched on by binding to short stretches of DNA, and evidence for those binding events is scattered across many experiments, assays and repositories. TFBSpedia collects that evidence into one place, maps it onto a common set of genomic regions, and records for every region which transcription factors bind it, which biological samples it was observed in, and how much supporting evidence stands behind it.

The purpose is to let a researcher start from a single question — where does this transcription factor bind? or what binds this region of the genome? — and get a complete, citable answer without having to download and reconcile the underlying resources themselves.

Where the data comes from

Binding sites are gathered from established public resources rather than generated here, then harmonised onto one coordinate system per assembly so that a region reported by two resources becomes a single entry carrying both lines of evidence.

Measured versus predicted sites

The two kinds of evidence are kept distinct throughout the database, and you can filter on them in the Advanced Search Options:

  • TFBS (ChIP-seq) — sites measured directly. A ChIP-seq experiment pulls down one specific transcription factor and sequences the DNA it was bound to, so the factor's identity is experimentally established.
  • Predicted TFBS (ATAC-seq, DNase-seq) — sites inferred from open chromatin. These assays show where the DNA is accessible and where a protein leaves a footprint, but not which protein; the transcription factor is assigned by matching its sequence motif. Because motifs are scanned in every accessible sample, predicted sites are far more numerous and far less specific than measured ones.

A single region can be supported by more than one assay and by more than one source database. That is deliberate — agreement between independent lines of evidence is what the confidence score measures — but it does mean per-assay and per-source counts add up to more than the total number of regions.

Region annotations

Every region is additionally intersected with a set of functional annotations, which are shown on the detail page for that region and feed into its importance score: promoters, enhancers, histone modification peaks, candidate cis-regulatory elements, regulatory element–gene links, transposable elements, variable CpG sites, GWAS associations, eQTLs, Codebook ChIP and GHT-SELEX experiments, and blacklisted regions.

Searching and downloading

1. Search for a transcription factor or a region

The search box on the home page accepts either kind of query:

  • a transcription factor name — ESR1, CTCF, FOXP3 (autocomplete suggests names as you type)
  • a genomic interval — chr1,10000,20000
  • a whole chromosome — chr1

Choose Human or Mouse beneath the box. Under Advanced Search Options you can restrict results to one cell line or tissue, and to measured or predicted sites only.

2. Search many terms at once

Switch the toggle to File Upload to run a batch search. Upload a .txt or .csv file with one term per line — TF names and genomic regions can be mixed in the same file. Up to 1,000 terms per file, 10 MB maximum.

3. Read the results table

Results appear as a paginated table of regions, one row per region, with its chromosome, start and end coordinates. The Actions column opens a detail page for that region showing every transcription factor bound there, the samples it was seen in, its scores, and all overlapping annotations.

4. Download the full result set

Underneath the results table there is a “Download All Results” button. This is the part worth understanding clearly:

The button downloads every matching region, not just the page you are looking at. The table shows 25 rows at a time; the CSV contains the complete result set for your query and filters, which for a well-studied factor can be more than a million rows and several tens of megabytes.

The file starts downloading within about a second and then streams, so a large download will show as “in progress” in your browser for a while. That is expected — let it finish rather than cancelling and retrying.

The CSV has one row per region and these columns:

  • Chromosome, Start, End — the region's coordinates
  • ID — the region's stable TFBSpedia identifier, which you can use to look it up again
  • Confident_Score and Important_Score — the two scores described below

Batch searches have the same button on their results page. Note that regions sharing identical coordinates are reported once, so the number of rows in the file can be slightly smaller than the number of entries reported above the table.

5. Download a transcription factor list

The Downloads menu in the top bar provides the full list of transcription factors held for each assembly, as a CSV giving each factor's name and how many regions it binds in total, by ChIP-seq, and by prediction.

The two scores

Every region carries two integer scores, both reported in the results table, on the region detail page, and in every CSV download.

  • Confident score — how well supported the binding site is, counting the number of independent source databases plus the number of sequencing techniques that report it. Higher means more independent evidence. Range 0–8 (human), 0–4 (mouse).
  • Important score — how much regulatory significance the region carries, combining overlap with promoters and enhancers, DNA methylation variability across tissues, Codebook annotations, GWAS and eQTL variants, and histone modification peaks. Higher means more regulatory evidence. Range 0–7 (human), 0–4 (mouse).
